Core Musculature Anatomy

Understanding the front and back pillars of your core helps you activate the right muscles during movement.

Anterior (Front) View

Posterior (Back) View

Rectus Abdominis

Function: Flexes the lumbar spine forward and helps tilt the pelvis posteriorly during dynamic movements like sitting up or sprinting.

Joints Protected: Stabilizes the lumbar spine and sacroiliac (SI) joints against excessive hyperextension under load.

Obliques (Internal & External)

Function: Control trunk rotation, lateral side-bending, and anti-rotation forces when carrying uneven loads or swinging.

Joints Protected: Shields the thoracic and lumbar intervertebral discs from shear and rotational strain.

Transverse Abdominis

Function: Acts as the body's natural deep weightlifting belt by compressing the abdominal cavity and increasing intra-abdominal pressure.

Joints Protected: Provides foundational segmental stability to the lumbar vertebrae and pelvis before limb movement begins.

Gluteus (Maximus & Medius)

Function: Drives hip extension, abduction, and pelvic leveling during walking, running, squatting, and climbing stairs.

Joints Protected: Protects the hip joints, lower back, and knees by preventing inward knee collapse and lumbar compensation.

Erector Spinae

Function: Extends and straightens the vertebral column while controlling forward flexion when bending or lifting objects.

Joints Protected: Supports the entire spinal column and facet joints, maintaining healthy upright posture against gravity.
